# My Travel Blog

The places I have been, and my diary from the road.

## Japan

### June 20, 2026
... That was one long plane ride. All the way from Chicago ORD to Tokyo HND, and a direct flight, too. As soon as I got off, I was glad to be walking around and moving my feet again. It was raining heavily. I had to take a bus to my hotel.

Eventually, I made it. After spending some quality time with family, I decided to venture outdoors on my own. The hotel was a 20 minute walk from central Shinjuku, so I decided to go exploring that way. It was such a unique place, unlike anything I'd ever seen before. I was very tired, so I crashed early.

### June 21, 2026
We woke up early and went to Asakusa to see the Senso-ji temple. It was pretty amazing - the only thing I've seen similar to that was a Tibetan buddhist temple in Singapore. The whole complex was a pleasure to walk in and around. After exploring the temple, we walked along the streets of Asakusa, and got some sweet bread and Hokkaido milk tea ice cream from some of the local vendors. They both tasted amazing.

For lunch, we went to a Vegan Ramen spot. After filling our stomachs to the brim, we went back to our hotel near Shinjuku. I wasn't tired, thought, so I ventured alone to Shinjuku proper to check out some of the department stores and possibly go shopping and get some nice trinkets. I didn't end up finding anything I liked, though, unfortunately, so I went back to the hotel empty-handed. In the evening, we took the subway to Roppongi to explore the Roppongi Hills area and grab dinner. We spent some time in a local bookstore, and then went to a pizzeria for dinner. The pizza was surprisingly good! (which was something I didn't expect 😂)

### June 22, 2026
The weather app said it was going to rain today, so we chose an indoor activity to do for the morning. We ended up going to the TeamLab Planets Tokyo exhibit, and what a sight it was. We walked through a waterway to a dim glow of light, and through rooms surrounded by Orchids growing all around us. We made our way around large, neon-colored, inflated spheres, and immersed ourselves in a virtual flower garden. It was frankly as if we became part of an art piece ourselves.

We had vegan food again for lunch, and eventually made our way to the Ginza shopping district. After getting a massive haul from Uniqlo, we found a local matcha store and got some nice drinks and tea from there. It was honestly quite amazing. I decided to stay back in Ginza for a little bit longer, and walked Northwest, until I eventually found myself at the Central Tokyo Station. I walked around the station through the malls and stores there, and bought a load of merch for myself and my friends back home. 

That evening, we were determined to see the Shibuya crossing, since we were scheduled to leave Tokyo the next day. After a hearty dinner, we made our way there and eventually found ourselves at the crossing. We crossed every possible crossing of two points at that intersection at least twice, and spent an hour meticulously inspecting every store nearby and looking at the people-counter board. I feel like if I were doing my PhD in that city, people-watching at this crossing might have become one of my favorite pastimes.

### June 23, 2026
We woke up early in the morning to get to Tokyo station so we could catch a train around noon to Kyoto. I went to a local store in the station and had some nice Tsukemen (which is a dish I had only had once before, in the Arts District in LA of all places) and it was quite good!

We then lined up at the station. While waiting, we got some nice ice cream at one of the vending machines there. The train ride to Kyoto was just over two hours. Apparently you can see Mt. Fuji some days, but it was too cloudy the day we went, so we unfortunately weren't able to catch a glimpse of it. After reaching Kyoto and getting out of the train, I was shocked to see how different it was from Tokyo! While Tokyo felt like a high-rise, metropolitan city, Kyoto felt like a city with several old, traditional homes and a lot of history. It was a sight to walk around!

## Eastern Sierra Road Trip

### March 16, 2026

We got up early in the morning to begin our journey. After filling up gas in my family's beautiful Toyota Prius, we stopped by the Breakfast Republic at UTC for a quick meal, where I got a nice egg benedict dish with avocado. We then began our long journey up, first hitting the 91, then the 15, and eventually the main road we were going to drive on - the US 395. Though the drive started off boring, with vast stretches of desert on either side of us, as we approached the northern part of San Bernadino County, the southernmost mountains of the Sierra Nevada came into view, and we saw them tower into the sky in front of us! Our first stop was the Fossil Falls rock formation in the Coso range just north of Inyokern. Stopping the car at the road nearby, we took a short hike until we reached the volcanic rock formation, which was far bigger than I thought.

We then continued our road trip up the 395 through the Owens Valley. Straddled by the Sierra Nevada on the West and the Coso range on the east, the view from the car was amazing on all sides, and we eventually saw snow-capped peaks very nearby. We eventually reached Lone Pine, our destination for the night, and checked into the cozy motel nearby. After a nice, relaxing rest and eating some tacos from the absolutely bomb food truck nearby, we journeyed west to the Alabama Hills. We spent the sunset gazing at Mt. Whitney, the highest mountain in the continental U.S., through the Mobius Arch in the hills. We then enjoyed a nice drive back home. Determined to see the sunrise from the Mobius Arch the next day since we'd heard so much about it, we retired early so we could wake up on time to see it.

### March 17, 2026
We got up at 4:30 AM today and immediately jumped in the car to head back to the Alabama hills. We hiked to the Mobius Arch and got there at 5:30, on time to witness (in the company of several friendly photographers from places as far as Germany) the breathtaking sunrise. It still sits on my memory as one of the best sunrises I've ever seen. 

Heading back to the motel, we quickly checked out and resumed our drive up the 395. Passing the Manzanar camp and the city of Bishop, where we went to a Denny's for brunch, we eventually started approaching Lake Crowley. After a 45-minute hike, we were able to see and touch the Crowley Lake stone columns. They were so interesting, and reminded me of pillars and arches I've seen in photos of ancient temples on the Eastern Mediterranean. The water was crystal clear, too, so the reflection of the pillars in the lake made them look almost like a the teeth of the whale. After touching a few of them and getting some nice pictures, we hiked back to our car and drove up to our next destination - Mammoth Lakes. As we entered deeper and deeper into Mono County, we saw snow creeping closer and closer until eventually, there was snow on the roadside. The surroundings also got much greener, as we left the Mojave desert and entered into a forest. 

After checking into our hotel at Mammoth Lakes, we drove around and tried to see as many of the actual lakes as we could. We walked around the twin lakes and some of the other lower altitude lakes, and I was amazed at the snowy forest and mountains I was surrounded by and how clear the water underneath me was. The air was crisp and fresh, almost similar to the air I've smelled in Kerala during the monsoon season, but colder and not as sticky. We weren't able to access the higher altitude lakes (due to road closures), so we went back and explored the town. We did try to see if we could get a lift to the top of Mammoth mountain, but ultimately decided against it (it was so expensive!). Towards the evening, we found ourselves at a local tiki bar, enjoying some nice, unique Mammoth cocktails before we crashed for the night.

### March 18, 2026
Grabbing a couple of energy drinks at the local Vons, we started the drive up to Lee Vining and Mono Lake. Once we got there, we walked to the lake shore and were amazed at the salt formations just sitting on the water. We then went to the Mono Lake Visitor Center, and did the hardest hike possible over there to another part of the lake shore. The sight of the lake full of salt formations surrounded by the Sierra Nevada and the Great Basin desert in the background really was a crazy sight to see - I almost felt as if I were on a different planet.

Turning around here, we made our way south on the 395, but wanted to take a different route back that wasn't endless desert. So once we got back to Inyokern, we took a detour on the 14 and made our way to the Red Rock Canyon state park. The pillars there made me think of the settlements on Tatooine from Star Wars or Arrakis from Dune. We climbed up a few arches and found some high-up spots to gaze at the Mojave around us. After getting some nice pictures, we then decided to take the 178 through Lake Isabella all the way to Bakersfield. If I'm being honest, this might be the drive I've hated doing the most in my life and maybe the only highway I will never drive again - the roads were so thin and there was a giant cliff right next to us, so every second I spent on that road was filled with dread. We eventually reached Bakersfield, checked into a motel for the night, and crashed after a hearty buffet.

### March 19, 2026
We rose early today because we were determined to leave Bakersfield sooner rather than later. The drive down south was scenic, and eventually, we reached the beautiful city of Malibu. We first toured Pepperdine University, and I was amazed at the ocean views from practically every part of campus. The campus itself was beautiful too, with massive recreation centers and areas to go for nice walks and runs in. We grabbed a late lunch at a nice Mediterranean restaurant, and eventually made our way back to the LA metro area.
